# Visual benchmarks and what to learn from them

Read when establishing a new direction or diagnosing a generic result. These
are visual observations, not conversion findings. Inspect the actual images
available to the task; do not pretend the notes are a visual reference. Learn
the relationships and craft, not a fixed palette or layout recipe.

## Portfolio sample inspected on 2026-11-13

The following public shots were opened and their artwork viewed from
[Scrnsht Studio's portfolio](https://dribbble.com/scrnshtstudio). Observations
refer to visible panels in the published shots, not unseen full campaigns.
Third-party images are linked, not bundled in this package.

### TextPort: transformation made physical

[Public shot](https://dribbble.com/shots/27870023-App-Store-Screenshots-App-Store-Preview-TextPort)

A blue campaign uses condensed heavy type, a pink scanning band and a hand
holding the exported conversation on paper. The next frame enlarges the export
menu and reverses the headline/device order. File-format tiles are objects with
edges and shadows; app tiles lead toward documents in another frame.

**Transfer:** make an input/output relationship visible, and give a utility a
material vocabulary. The printout has a narrative job. Don't add a hand, scanner
beam or file tiles to an app that does not support those actions. Exporting
capability and numerical superiority remain separate factual claims.

### CarSnap: the same subject through the journey

[Public shot](https://dribbble.com/shots/27870009-App-Store-Screenshots-App-Store-Preview-CarSnap)

A silver car dominates a light opening scene and continues into the next panel,
where a hand-held camera frames it beneath an identification result. Later dark
panels show the collection. Pink acts as a controlled identity accent. The
photographic car, camera subject and result have a direct relationship.

**Transfer:** continuity can be semantic as well as panoramic: carry one item
through capture, result and collection. Contrast marks a change in the story.
A car photo beside an unrelated phone would lose the mechanism.

### Shelf: editorial collage as product identity

[Public shot](https://dribbble.com/shots/27471928-App-Store-Screenshots-App-Store-Preview-Shelf)

A close, expressive portrait sits among cultural artifacts. Tall narrow type
mixes light and heavy weights. Orange, acid green and pale panels vary while
cutout edges, labels and collectible cover shapes maintain continuity. Discs,
books and portraits emerge from the relevant interface groups.

**Transfer:** select content that expresses the audience's identity and carry
its graphic treatment into the UI presentation. The collage's personality comes
from casting, crop and typography, not an arbitrary quantity of stickers.

### Blank Spaces: restrained UI, dramatic staging

[Public shot](https://dribbble.com/shots/27133978-App-Store-Screenshots-App-Store-Preview-Blank-Spaces)

A very simple black text interface becomes a large device seen in receding
perspective across frames. Serif and heavy sans type contrast against purple
and pink illumination. The quiet screen has scale and hardware presence rather
than a conventional centered full-phone thumbnail.

**Transfer:** sparse product content can support dramatic framing. An anti-slop
rule banning gradients or large perspective would remove useful choices here.
Still verify individual crops and substantiate any comparison or time-saving claim.

### CosmosTune: a human mood joined to product behavior

[Public shot](https://dribbble.com/shots/27694758-App-Store-Screenshots-App-Store-Preview-CosmosTune)

A softly lit, closed-eye listener leads into a black interface and pastel audio
tiles. Fine colored edges on type repeat across panels. The background-listening
frame pairs a player with another activity on the screen. Soft fades establish
depth and atmosphere without competing with the foreground content.

**Transfer:** emotion and functional explanation can share one visual world.
The portrait has a listening context; an arbitrary model photo would be weaker.
Do not treat the artwork's wellness language as evidence of efficacy.

### Serenity: consistency of texture, not just color

[Public shot](https://dribbble.com/shots/27694713-App-Store-Screenshots-App-Store-Preview-Serenity)

Blue grain, soft illustrated bears, hazy interface edges, serif headlines and
handwritten secondary lines create a gentle world. A path and course imagery
change the composition while the same surface treatment joins illustrations,
background and UI. The secondary text has a clear subordinate rank.

**Transfer:** texture, typography and illustration can do more for identity than
another hardware pose. Don't transplant its bear or blur essential UI. A blanket
ban on subtitles, grain or decorative illustration would flatten the result.

## Earlier reference families

The supplied habit-game, expert-marketplace, travel-connectivity and form-builder
examples add other useful mechanisms: tactile rewards linked to a character;
human editorial portraits joined by a ribbon; a globe and flight path that
establish connectivity; and a paper plane associated with a sharing action.
The earlier driving-school campaign used a road, clock, calendar and hour bars
with one consistent blue material, lighting and realistic hardware. Those
objects filled and shaped the scene while the enlarged UI remained legible.
Transfer their construction logic, not the road, color or mascot to every app.

The [seven-principle article](https://x.com/scrnshtstudio/status/2090816024585089129)
([accessible mirror with images](https://maxed.wiki/posts/how-to-make-high-converting-app-store-screenshots-for-your-app-7-principles/))
also shows useful semantic continuity: Keepbox carries the same drawing from
paper into storage; WalkBlock joins walking to unlock progress. Clarity is one
part of the benchmark. Type, imagery and execution still need separate inspection.

## Diagnose the gap without creating a new house template

| Weak result | Why a technically correct result can still disappoint | A more useful correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bold UI font, phone, flat color, one enlarged card | Hierarchy exists, but type and composition have little character | Choose type shape and a subject relationship from this app's world; design their crop and proportions together |
| Different phone angles in each frame | Rotation changes geometry without advancing the story | Vary the focal content or its role: context, action, result, collection; keep repeated poses when helpful |
| Large duplicated article over a news feed | The duplicate hides useful UI and reads as a pasted tile | Let selected content and its context organize the scene; plan how the image continues, reveals or relates to the feed |
| Giant alert card between unrelated empty areas | The card reads, but the whole frame feels assembled | Integrate the action, background and type through intentional scale, overlap and consistent finish |
| Device ends at a hard color boundary above a tiny event row | The bottom region feels like a separate flyer | Recompose the event image, time and device as one visual group; give the actual event a useful share of the frame |
| Beautiful isolated prop beside unrelated UI | The rendering is competent but the story is missing | Establish a specific connection, or change the subject; adding a caption rarely repairs it |
| All details stripped to avoid AI-looking embellishment | Sparse output is mistaken for art direction | Restore the texture, context or material the chosen concept needs; judge integration and hierarchy |

Use these as diagnostic examples, not bans. The target is a purposeful, finished
campaign. Neither more effects nor fewer objects automatically gets it there.
