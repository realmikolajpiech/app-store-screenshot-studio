# Art direction: design the campaign's world

## Study the pictures before choosing a treatment

Inspect the user's references as images, including individual panels and the
whole strip. For a portfolio, sample contrasting campaigns and open their art;
do not infer a designer's style from titles, tags or a single thumbnail.
[visual-benchmarks.md](visual-benchmarks.md) contains worked observations and
links, not a substitute for seeing the images available in the current task.

For the closest references, identify the visible decisions that create the
effect: type shape and emphasis, subject/crop, proportions, overlaps, light,
material, color distribution and transitions between frames. Separate:

- **Transferable craft:** e.g. a physical object and its scanned result share
  the same identity; a purchase photo carries its amount; text wraps a silhouette.
- **This campaign's vocabulary:** its font personality, characters, surface,
  photographic direction and energy. Choose a compatible vocabulary for the app.
- **Non-transferable content:** another app's artwork, identities, claims,
  integration logos or features. A reference is not permission or product evidence.

Do not collect ingredients from unrelated references into one design. Choose a
primary visual direction and use secondary references for specific craft questions.

## Choose a world, a subject and a relationship

Read the app's colors, type, imagery, corners, density and tone. Extend these into
a campaign rather than merely using its accent color on a default poster. A
functional interface can support a richer environment while remaining recognizable.
Choose the direction because of the product, audience and supplied references.
For example, a sports app may support athletic editorial photography; a playful
habit app a tactile character world; a reader a human listening moment; a utility
a dramatically enlarged, precisely composed action. These are options, not presets.

Write a short art brief containing:

| Decision | Be specific enough to draw it |
| --- | --- |
| Promise and feeling | What this audience gets; what the image should feel like |
| Hero subject | The particular screen region, content, person or object; its source |
| Visual relationship | How subject, UI and type interact: held, scanned, revealed, wrapped, stacked, emerging, compared |
| Typographic character | Width, weight contrast, case, emphasis and intended line shape; actual available font |
| Spatial/material language | Flat editorial, photographic, illustrated or dimensional; crop, depth, light and surfaces |
| Brand anchors | What stays recognizable from the app; what creative extension is appropriate |

“Large phone, bold heading, floating card” describes a layout inventory. It
doesn't explain why those elements belong together. “A handwritten page becomes
the same saved item visible in the phone” establishes a relationship. A textured
scene can also establish calm, energy or tactility without being a literal diagram.
State its emotional role and connect it to the product experience.

For an open direction, explore different ideas, not three colors or angles of
the same idea. Use the real focal content in rough layouts before investing in
finished supporting art. Do not substitute a wireframe or asset inventory for
the finished visual check required before producing the full set.

## Give type a designed role

Compose the words and picture together. Consider the headline's silhouette,
weight distribution and relationship to the main subject. A tight italic word,
wide heavy line, narrow headline, serif contrast or handwritten accent can be
appropriate when it belongs to the brand. Choose intentionally; a default bold
UI font on every campaign loses much of the reference work's character.

Emphasize the meaningful word rather than automatically coloring the last line.
Use natural localized copy and a font with the required glyphs. Adjust phrasing,
font width or layout before shrinking a headline into small type. Check optical
spacing, line breaks, diacritics and awkward isolated words in the actual render.

One dominant message does not mean one text element. A feature label can orient,
a short caption explain a mechanism, and real proof reassure. Give each a clear
rank. Remove filler, repetition and unsupported claims, not useful context merely
because the layout contains several lines. No fixed word, line or font quota.

## Make the layers belong to the same picture

Use scale and cropping to select what matters. The entire phone need not be
visible; its useful region should be. An interface group may be amplified into
the foreground, but avoid a contradictory duplicate or a card that obscures the
very interaction being sold. Plan whether a breakout replaces a small detail,
continues the screen or isolates an outcome.

Choose a coherent relationship between foreground, product and background.
In a dimensional scene, match perspective, light direction, contact shadows,
reflections and color spill. In a flat collage, coordinate cutout treatment,
edge quality, overlap and graphic scale. A cast shadow alone does not integrate
a pasted asset. Avoid near-tangencies between type, bezels and protruding cards;
make overlaps deliberate and keep the focal words and controls readable.

Photography needs casting and a crop: a face, gesture, place or object should
contribute use context, desire, identity or emotion. A random skyline behind a
local feed fails because the relationship is weak, not because scenery is banned.
Use a recognizable product story or interaction to connect a contextual image.
An event's own photo may carry its appeal; an unrelated city photo cannot replace it.

Atmosphere is allowed. Gradients, grain, glass, 3D, stickers, glows and decorative
objects are not intrinsically good or bad. Keep them when their treatment fits
the visual world and strengthens emphasis or feeling. Avoid stock embellishment
added solely to make an underdesigned composition seem elaborate.
When consulting general UI taste guidance, transfer its critical judgment;
do not import website-specific font bans, layout randomization, animation rules
or blanket effect bans into a screenshot campaign.

## Direct the sequence as a whole

### Background test after rendering

A supplied logo establishes identity; it does not automatically justify turning
its geometry into giant scenery. Before expanding the first two frames, name
what the background visibly contributes: usage context, explanation, emotional
setting or deliberate graphic hierarchy. Point to the rendered relationship,
not the prompt's intention. If the only explanation is “it matches the logo” or
“it looks premium,” reduce it or choose a stronger concept. Neutral space is
valid; not every frame needs an illustrated scene or a physical prop.

Check whether the decoration could be substituted behind every feature without
changing its role. Repetition may unify a series, but it must not become the
entire concept. A change of font, color or device lean does not resolve a rejected
visual idea. After such feedback, compare the revised opening and contrasting
frame with the reference again before producing the rest.

Observed revision failure: feedback requesting a different font and brand palette
was treated as a replacement brief. The next series repeated logo-derived scenery
and device staging, losing the earlier requirements for meaningful scenes,
varied hierarchy and a composed sequence. Preserve those requirements explicitly
when applying the requested changes. Neither logo geometry nor atmospheric
backgrounds are inherently wrong; fixation on the latest input is the failure.

Plan each frame's message, visual idea, focal source, type shape, dominant mass,
crop, contrast and transition to its neighbors. Choose the number and order from
the product story; do not force a six-step feature template.

A strong opening can lead with an outcome, human moment, content or action.
Subsequent frames can reveal the mechanism, enlarge a result or open up breadth.
The same type, materials and motif can tie these together without identical
hierarchy. Conversely, repeated upright devices can provide useful calm when
their content and surrounding scene carry the progression. Do not force
left/right rotation or a new color in every frame to manufacture variety.

Honor explicit requests for varied poses or a phone in every frame. A panoramic
device or shared scene can connect frames when the placement supports it; check
individual crops as well as the strip. Keep a frame's essential message usable
when viewed alone. Don't rely on a seamless montage that the store won't display.

Before expanding a new series, finish the opener and a contrasting frame. Inspect
them beside a relevant reference at comparable size. A good direction must
support both the hook and a useful product explanation. Fix the idea if the
image lacks a compelling subject; fix the execution if the idea is strong but
the type, assets or depth still look rough. More badges won't solve either.
